Building is celebrated. Maintenance is ignored. Yet maintenance is where ownership becomes real. When you create something worth having, you inherit the responsibility of preserving it. A body built in the gym is not a permanent object; it is a condition that must be repeatedly defended. Stop training for three months and the body changes. That is not a flaw. That is the contract. The same pattern applies to knowledge, relationships, money, skill, and identity. Everything valuable decays without attention. 

Maintenance is the hidden cost that sits beneath all ownership. This is why many people fail to appreciate the true burden of success. They imagine the peak as a destination, when in fact it is a maintenance problem. The better the thing, the more care it requires. That does not make it not worth having. It simply means ownership is active, not passive. A serious life is not just about acquisition. It is about preservation, renewal, and upkeep. 

If you do not maintain what you build, entropy eventually collects its debt. Maintenance is therefore not secondary. It is part of the achievement itself.
