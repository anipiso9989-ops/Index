Order matters, but life is not reducible to a war against entropy. That would be too narrow. The universe may trend toward disorder in the abstract, but human life is not merely a physics problem. Some things require structure. Some require change. Some require disorder to produce adaptation. So the point is not to worship order or demonize entropy. The point is to organize reality toward a goal. That is what systems do. They are not just anti-chaos devices. They are tools for maintaining and arranging elements so they can serve a purpose. Simplicity is part of that, but not all of it. A good system makes existing order more manageable, more usable, and more durable. It reduces confusion without flattening nuance. It creates a framework in which the relevant details can be handled without drowning in them. In that sense, systems are not about freezing life into rigidity. They are about giving life enough structure to function. Order is valuable when it is purposeful. Entropy is not evil when it is constructive. The real question is not order versus disorder. The real question is whether the arrangement serves the goal.
