# Cognitive Models of Intelligence

Intelligence can be analyzed by contrasting two distinct cognitive models:

### System A (Static/Pattern-Matching)
- Memorizes large volumes of information.
- Recognizes existing patterns.
- Predicts subsequent states based on historical data.
- Solves familiar problems within known domains.

### System B (Dynamic/Derivational)
- Starts with minimal prior knowledge.
- Encounters novel, out-of-distribution problems.
- Formulates novel approaches to solve them.
- Invents useful abstractions.
- Learns from feedback and results.
- Transfers discoveries to unrelated domains.

System B represents a higher order of intelligence due to its reliance on two primary cognitive abilities: **derivation** and **generalization**.

### Derivation vs. Storage
An intelligent system does not require exhaustive knowledge storage. Instead, it must possess the capacity to derive information dynamically as needed. This is computationally more efficient than storing every potential state or answer.

### Generalization
Once information is derived, the system must generalize it to the widest logically valid range. 
- **Universal Generalization:** Rules that apply without boundary limits (e.g., the associative property).
- **Bounded Generalization:** Rules that are valid only within specific boundary conditions. An intelligent system must identify where generalization ceases to be logically valid.
