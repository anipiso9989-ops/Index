# Provisional Theory of Intelligence

This note synthesizes the core mathematical models and propositions of the intelligence framework.

### 1. Mathematical Formulations

#### The Vector Model of Intelligence
$$ \text{Intelligence} \sim \text{Capability directed toward a Goal} $$
- **Capability** (Magnitude)
- **Goal** (Direction)

#### The Model of Understanding
$$ U(X) = C(X) + I(X) + T(X) $$
- $C$ = Constituents
- $I$ = Interactions
- $T$ = Transformations/Dynamics

#### The Generalization Function
$$ \text{Specific derived structure} \rightarrow \text{Broader valid structure} $$
Subject to the logical conditions under which the structure remains true.

#### The Abstraction Function
$$ A(D, G) \rightarrow R $$
Where $D$ is raw data, $G$ is the goal, and $R$ is the reduced representation containing only the structure necessary for reasoning.

---

### 2. Core Propositions

- **Proposition 1 (Directed Intelligence):** Intelligence is raw cognitive capability given functional utility by a goal.
- **Proposition 2 (Derivational Efficiency):** Intelligence does not require complete information storage; it requires the capacity to derive information dynamically as needed.
- **Proposition 3 (Constrained Generalization):** Intelligence requires maximizing the scope of derived rules while explicitly defining the logical boundaries where those rules fail.
- **Proposition 4 (Goal-Dependent Relevance):** The relevance of information is not intrinsic; it is determined by the system's objective function. Abstraction is therefore goal-relative.
- **Proposition 5 (Structured Understanding):** Understanding a system requires representing its components, their interactions, and their transformations over time.
- **Proposition 6 (Functional Output):** A system with high computational capacity but no output or action lacks the functional definition of intelligence.
