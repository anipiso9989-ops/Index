# Generalization and Its Limits

An intelligent system must determine the boundaries of its generalizations to prevent logical failure.

### Naive vs. Intelligent Generalization

- **Naive Generalization:** Assumes that because a rule works in a specific instance, it applies universally.
- **Intelligent Generalization:** Identifies a rule's validity in a specific instance, determines the precise logical conditions under which that rule remains true, and generalizes strictly within those boundaries.

### Logical Derivation of Conditions
To determine the boundaries of a derived rule, a system must derive the necessary conditions that must be satisfied for the rule to remain true:

$$ \text{Derived rule} \rightarrow \text{derive necessary conditions} \rightarrow \text{determine validity} \rightarrow \text{generalize within those conditions} $$

### The Premise Bottleneck
While formal logic can easily derive consequences from predefined axioms (as in mathematics), real-world reasoning faces a fundamental bottleneck: **the origin of premises**. 

Logic dictates what follows from represented information, but it cannot determine which facts about the physical world should be represented in the first place. This necessitates a mechanism for relevance and abstraction.
