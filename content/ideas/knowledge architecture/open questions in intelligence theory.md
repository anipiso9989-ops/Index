# Open Questions in Intelligence Theory

The following theoretical gaps remain unresolved within the current framework:

### A. The Irreducible Operation of Intelligence
What is the single, fundamental operation that underlies:
- reasoning,
- derivation,
- abstraction,
- generalization, and
- understanding?
Can these processes be reduced to a single primitive cognitive mechanism, or is intelligence inherently multi-faceted?

### B. The Scope of Reasoning
Is "reasoning" a sufficiently broad concept to encompass every cognitive operation classified as intelligent? If not, what is the more fundamental term for cognitive action?

### C. The Status of Components
Are components truly irreducible primitives, or can everything be represented purely as relationships and transformations? 

### D. The Status of Transformations
Can dynamics/transformations be derived entirely from $\text{components} + \text{interactions}$, or must transformation be treated as an independent primitive?

### E. The Status of Function
Can function be derived entirely from $\text{components} + \text{interactions} + \text{dynamics} + \text{goal/context}$, or does it require independent status?

### F. The Origin of Goals
If goals provide the direction of intelligence, what system or meta-heuristic generates, selects, and updates the goals themselves?

### G. Intelligence vs. Agency
Does intelligence strictly imply agency?
$$ \text{intelligence} \Rightarrow \text{agency} $$
Or can there exist highly intelligent, non-agentic systems that perform derivation and abstraction without autonomous goal-directed action?
