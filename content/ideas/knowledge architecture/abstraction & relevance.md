# Abstraction and Relevance

When observing any physical phenomenon, a system is confronted with an infinite amount of data (e.g., an apple's color, mass, shape, air density, molecular structure, position, velocity). Intelligence requires filtering this data to prevent computational overload.

### Goal-Driven Abstraction
At the observational level, information is filtered based on utility:

> **Retain a detail only if it contributes to the understanding of the observed phenomenon; otherwise, discard it.**

### The Relativity of Relevance
Relevance is not an intrinsic property of data. It is determined by the system's objective:
- **Goal: Predict trajectory** $\rightarrow$ Mass and velocity are highly relevant; color is irrelevant.
- **Goal: Image identification** $\rightarrow$ Color and shape are highly relevant; air density is irrelevant.
- **Goal: Aerodynamic modeling** $\rightarrow$ Shape and air density are highly relevant.

Therefore:

> **The objective function of an intelligent system determines what structure is considered relevant.**

Abstraction is inherently goal-dependent. A representation is not optimal because it captures the maximum amount of reality, but because it captures the precise structure required for the system's specific purpose.
