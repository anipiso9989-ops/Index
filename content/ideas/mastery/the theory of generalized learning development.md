True genius may not be raw IQ, but **highly adaptive cognitive flexibility** — the ability to rapidly recognize, transfer, and manipulate structure across radically different domains.

Mathematics, science, languages, music, and physical training are all systems of structured information:

- **Mathematics** trains abstraction and formal logic.
- **Science** trains causal reasoning and reality-modeling.
- **Polyglot language learning** trains adaptive thinking and cognitive flexibility.
- **Instrument training** trains pattern recognition, creativity, timing, and abstract expression.
- **Elite physical training** trains nervous-system efficiency, emotional regulation, precision under pressure, and execution speed.

Simultaneous training across these domains creates controlled cognitive friction. The brain is repeatedly forced to switch rule systems, reorganize attention, and transfer patterns between disciplines. Over time, this develops heightened intellectual plasticity, generalized problem-solving ability, and deep pattern recognition across reality itself. Attribution to this theory goes to Mark Bashmakov and Dmitry Faddeev, who built an elite school during the Cold War in the USSR to train gifted children using an intensive regimen to enhance their general learning ability.

However, more subjects ≠ automatically more intelligence. The key is **deep structured mastery**, not just exposure volume. Keep the stimulus within reason. 

The curriculum proposed within this framework:

- **Mathematics** → everything through undergraduate university mathematics
- **Science** → university-level physics, chemistry, or biology (only one of the 3 is required)
- **Languages** → at least 3 studied simultaneously
- **Music** → at least 2 instruments simultaneously, preferably classical
- **Physical Training** → sprinting, strength training, coordination, mobility, endurance conditioning

The ultimate objective is the development of a mind capable of:
- rapid adaptation,
- deep abstraction,
- cross-domain synthesis,
- sustained concentration,
- creative problem solving,
- and elite execution under pressure.

Not specialization alone — but generalized intellectual dominance.
