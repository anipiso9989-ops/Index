I roughly define learning rate as the following (may need to refine the formula):
$$\text{Learning Rate} \approx \frac{\text{Concept Packaging} + \text{Retention} + \text{Processing Speed} + \text{Prior Structure} + \text{Feedback}}{\text{Interference}}$$
- Concept packaging is here → [[concept packaging]]
- Retention: understanding something once isn't executable mastery. You need the representation to become cheaply and reliably retrievable. Otherwise every application requires reconstructing it.
- Processing Speed: how rapidly you can parse information, retrieve relevant knowledge, manipulate it, test possibilities, and update your model. This matters enormously, especially when the material itself isn't the bottleneck.
- Prior Structure: new knowledge attaches to old knowledge. Someone with excellent foundations can learn something "in an hour" because they're actually leveraging thousands of previously constructed packages. This is why expertise compounds.
- Feedback: how quickly you discover _where your mental model is wrong_. Problems, predictions, explaining from memory, experiments, and immediate feedback accelerate this dramatically.
- Interference: uninterrupted cognitive bandwidth matters. Context switching, fatigue, anxiety, irrelevant information, and overloaded working memory effectively reduce the processing available for learning.

Fast thinking $\neq$ Fast learning. There's several parts to the equation.
