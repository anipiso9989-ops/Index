# Language = Theory + Application

Language has two fundamental parts: **theory** and **application**.

**Theory** is what the language _is_:

- **Vocabulary**: the words that carry meaning. Build this through thousands of flashcards.
- **Grammar**: the rules that organize those words into meaningful communication.
> **Master Grammatical Text:** Instead of learning grammar as isolated rules, learn it through a deliberately engineered parallel text (written in your starting language, in this case English, and your target language) that demonstrates as many core grammatical structures in meaningful context as the target language contains. By studying this text deeply until every construction is intuitive, grammar becomes an internalized pattern rather than a memorized rulebook. Vocabulary can then be added modularly and indefinitely without rebuilding the underlying system.

**Application** is how humans _use_ the language:

- **Input**: reading and listening.
- **Output**: writing and speaking.
- This is built primarily through repeated practice in each aspect.

Everything in language learning ultimately reduces to acquiring vocabulary and grammar, then applying them through reading, listening, writing, and speaking.
