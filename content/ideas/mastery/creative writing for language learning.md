This is very similar to the note about [[how to learn to read and write in any language]], but slightly tweaked. 

The core hypothesis is that you can master primarily the grammar of a language by synthesizing given resources into a short story. 

Essentially, you are given a grammar guide, and a dictionary in that language (technically English-Target Language), and then you create a short story. 

My rationale for this is that it would drive higher order synthesis by requiring active use of specific grammar rules and words within the structure of a narrative. However, it's very important to stop regularly and get feedback to make sure you're writing correctly.

I think this idea has some validity, and if I have time to explore it I'll run an experiment and document it on my GitHub. Things to measure include grammar accuracy, time required to write the story (let's say a short story is 40 standard pages), and ability to use that grammar which I learned in a completely new context *after* the initial experiment is done. 

The question I'm trying to answer is if this type of creative writing can significantly improve grammar acquisition, and whether it's better than isolated exercises.

Another thing. Maybe, just maybe, this exercise could be extrapolated and generalized beyond languages to other domains where you need to synthesize knowledge rather than just recognize it.
