I feel like as Gen Zs, we're starting to go back to analog, or at least get away from big tech.

Big tech offers convenience at the expense of ownership's demise. You won't own anything anymore. Take streaming for example. You have access to all these shows and movies, but you don't own them; you can't watch them on a long car ride knowing that you actually **own** them; you're just renting.

Digital access provides benefits, but these companies prioritize these platforms deliberately to control how media is commodified, creating an endless cycle of dependence, which is the exact opposite of [[self containment and self sufficiency]]. 

The disposable nature of fast paced media too, is what makes that same media completely worthless. These algorithms push people to produce more and more, rather than slowing down and thinking about whether it's valuable or not. It's a quantity > quality problem, and it feels really dystopian to me.

The personal significance of physical items, perhaps passed down through generations serve as vital connections to people of ages past, and these connections simply don't exist in the binary world of the digital era.
