## Open Problems

### 1. Learning Process
What fundamentally distinguishes a learning-caused capacity change from maturation/development?

Current candidate: some process must be causally necessary for the change; purely endogenous maturation does not qualify. Biological boundaries may be fuzzy.

### 2. Persistence Threshold
What structural property distinguishes a transient state change from a learned state? Can “persistent enough” be formalized without an arbitrary time threshold?

### 3. Capacity
What exactly counts as a new or modified capacity? Is every measurable behavioral improvement a capacity change, or are some merely temporary states/performance effects?

### 4. Understanding
Is understanding a distinct phenomenon from learning, a property of learned models, or a subset/degree of learning?

### 5. Model Ontology
Is “model” a fundamental property of cognition or simply the most useful explanatory abstraction over underlying processes?

### 6. Knowledge-Graph Reality
Does graph structure correspond to something objectively real about cognition, or is it primarily a useful theoretical representation?

### 7. Objective Measurement
How can learning be operationally identified and measured without confusing it with performance, memory, or temporary state effects?

### 8. Generalization
Does the theory apply equally to humans, animals, and artificial systems, and which components are substrate-independent?

### 9. Accuracy and Learning
Can we formalize the relationship between learning a model and learning a model that accurately represents reality?

## Methodological Standard

Further analysis should distinguish three levels:

### Observation
What appears empirically or phenomenologically to happen.

### Hypothesis / Model
An explanatory claim that organizes observations.

### Established Theory
A claim supported by logic, comparison to alternatives, operationalization, prediction, and empirical testing.

Current ideas are largely **candidate theory**, not established scientific fact.

For future development, each major claim should be tested by asking:
- Is it logically necessary?
- Does it explain observations better than alternatives?
- Does it generalize?
- Can it make novel predictions?
- Could evidence falsify it?

## Current Provisional Synthesis

> **Learning is a persistent internal state change acquired through a causally necessary process that creates or modifies a capacity.**
>
> **The internal change can be modeled as modification of an informational/functional structure (“model”), while the resulting capacity is what makes the change consequential.**
>
> **Use, teaching, and extension are major forms of evidence for the acquired capacity, not components of learning itself.**
>
> **Learning is not identical to memory, understanding, performance, accuracy, or permanent retention, although it interacts with all of them.**

This is a **provisional theory**. The next stage is formalization, operational definitions, comparison with existing theories, empirical predictions, testing, and revision.
