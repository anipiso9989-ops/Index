Reasoning is fundamentally the continual transformation of mental representations, not merely arriving at an answer.

- **Goal-Directed**: At a practical level, reasoning is directed toward a goal, but deeply it is about manipulating relations.
- **No Free Lunch**: Outputs cannot emerge from nothing; they are transformations or recombinations of existing inputs.
- **Operators**: Deduction, induction, and abduction are simply different transformation operators, not different phenomena.
- **Relational**: An isolated concept cannot reason. Reasoning requires understanding and manipulating relations between representations under constraints.

This is the cognitive equivalent of [[information transmutation]], where raw inputs are crystallized into higher forms of understanding.

But, this process of transformation is cannot be done in any way. 

Reasoning ability is distinct from stored knowledge. 
- **Reconstruction > Recall**: Someone with fewer memorized facts but a better understanding of fundamental principles can often reconstruct what someone else merely recalls. 
- **Core Drivers**: Reasoning is tied to the quality of representation, quality of transformation, and understanding of relations, rather than memory alone. 

This distinction supports the practice of [[selective forgetting]] to keep the mind lightweight, and shifts the focus of [[how to remember what you learn]] from rote storage to active, structural reconstruction. This is very similar to [[the minimum knowledge required]].
