### Capacity and Agency
The purpose/consequence of learning is gaining or modifying **ability/capacity**, which can be viewed as increased **agency**. Agency is domain-relative: even trivial information can create a small new capability (e.g. being able to discuss a TV show).

The stronger objective claim is not that learning makes life globally more useful, but that learning produces a new or modified capacity to act, distinguish, predict, explain, solve, or otherwise operate within some domain.

### Demonstrating a Learned Capacity
Three major forms of evidence:

1. **Use** — apply the model/capability, including to novel instances.
2. **Teach** — communicate the model sufficiently for another mind to acquire/use it.
3. **Extend** — generate genuinely new concepts or relationships from the model.

**Discovery = extension.** Novel application using existing principles is use, not extension.

These are not exhaustive and are not required simultaneously. A simple factual learning event may only need reliable use; richer models may support stronger forms of demonstration.
