The real goal is making the best decisions over the longest timeframe, not just a single good choice.

A single brilliant choice is worthless if it leads to later failures, whereas a cautious choice preserving future decision quality is highly valuable.

Good systems protect judgment, stabilize execution, and keep future options open rather than maximizing immediate results at all costs.

Process durability and consistency matter more than short-term intensity, meaning the system must improve with repetition instead of degrading under pressure.

Long horizons punish impulsive optimization and reward disciplined consistency.

The ultimate standard and target is a lifetime decision-making architecture, not an isolated best decision now.
