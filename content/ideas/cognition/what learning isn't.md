### What Learning Is Not
- Mere information exposure.
- Mere retention or memory.
- A transient state change.
- Demonstration itself.
- Guaranteed truth or accuracy.
- Necessarily practical or globally useful knowledge.
- Permanently retained knowledge.

### Key Distinctions
- **Learning vs. demonstration:** demonstration is evidence that learning occurred, not learning itself.
- **Learning vs. retention:** retention is persistence of acquired information; learning requires a consequential capacity change.
- **Learning vs. understanding:** learning concerns acquisition/modification of a model; understanding concerns knowledge of how concepts and relationships within the model interact. This distinction remains provisional.
- **Learning vs. maturation:** maturation can create capacity without a learning process being causally necessary. The boundary may be fuzzy in biological systems.
- **Learning vs. performance:** temporary constraints such as fatigue can mask a learned capacity without preventing learning.
- **Learning vs. forgetting:** something can be genuinely learned and later forgotten.
