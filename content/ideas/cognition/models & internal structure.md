## Definition of a Model
A **model** is an informational/functional structure representing what has been acquired. It is the current abstraction for the internal structure underlying a learned capability; it is not claimed to be the literal neural mechanism.

Models may be:
- informational, agentic/procedural, or both;
- explicit or implicit/embodied;
- composed of concepts, relationships, procedures, and organizational structure.

Implicit or embodied representations may be things the model uses rather than conventional conscious “mental models.”

## Concepts and Relationships
A **concept** can be treated as a standalone fact or piece of information.

A model can contain:
- a single concept,
- multiple concepts,
- relationships among concepts.

Relations cannot meaningfully exist without concepts, but concepts can participate in many relationships and models. A concept such as integration can function across many models.

Concepts can sometimes act as relational operators when combining concepts into larger structures.

## Model Boundaries
A model may be a practically defined **region of an individual's larger knowledge graph**, with soft boundaries.

The same knowledge could theoretically be represented as one enormous interconnected model. “Model” may therefore be a useful practical partition rather than a naturally discrete object.
