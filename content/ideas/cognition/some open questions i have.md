These are the fundamental open questions regarding the limits and mechanics of human cognition:

- **Q1. What fundamentally determines reasoning ability?** If knowledge, working memory, processing speed, and motivation are held constant, what property of the mind explains why one person consistently reasons better than another?
- **Q2. What makes one transformation "better" than another?** Is it better representations, better operators, better constraint management, or better search through possibility space?
- **Q3. What is the optimal representation?** What representation minimizes cognitive cost while maximizing expressiveness, composability, generalization, and ease of transformation?
- **Q4. What is the minimal unit of reasoning?** Current hypothesis: Not an isolated concept, but a relation between at least two representations.
- **Q5. How does genuinely novel reasoning emerge?** If every new idea is a recombination of prior concepts, what governs high-quality recombinations? Why do some minds discover elegant abstractions while others do not?
- **Q6. Can reasoning be reduced to first principles?** Can every form of reasoning—mathematical, scientific, philosophical, creative, strategic—be derived from a small set of primitive operations? If so, what are those primitives?
