### Provisional Definition
**Learning is a persistent internal state change acquired through a necessary process that creates or modifies a capacity.**

The internal change is currently modeled as a change in an informational/functional **model**. The resulting capacity is the consequential output of learning.

### Core Causal Chain
**Process / experience → internal state change → persistent model/structure → new or modified capacity → potential demonstration**

### Process Independence
The specific acquisition mechanism does not define whether learning occurred. Study, practice, observation, instruction, discovery, reasoning, experience, or artificial intervention can all qualify.

What matters is that some process is **causally necessary** for producing the persistent capacity change, distinguishing learning from capacity changes arising solely from maturation/development.

### Substrate Independence
Two systems can learn the same thing even if their physical implementations differ, provided their resulting functional/informational organization and external capabilities are equivalent enough for the learned content to count as the same.

This allows the theory to potentially generalize across biological and artificial systems.
