## Pure Information
Learning can concern extremely simple facts. A model does not need to be sophisticated or practically important to count as learned.

## Skills
Procedural and motor learning can involve implicit/embodied representations. The “model” is the functional structure supporting the skill, not necessarily a consciously articulated description of it.

## Practice
Repeated practice can count as learning when it produces a persistent capability/model change, even without acquiring new external information.

## Fatigue and Other Constraints
A learned capability can exist while temporary external/internal constraints suppress its expression. Therefore observed performance is not identical to learned capacity.

A learner should, absent the blocking constraint, be capable of demonstrating the changed capacity through use, teaching, or extension.

## Accidental Success
One lucky correct response does not establish learning. Demonstration matters as evidence because it helps distinguish a genuine changed capacity from chance performance.

## Forgetting and Relearning
Learning can be followed by forgetting without retroactively negating the original learning.

Previous learning may leave latent structure that facilitates relearning, but this is an empirical hypothesis rather than a settled component of the theory.

## Unlearning / Model Updating
“Unlearning” may be better understood as **model updating** in response to evidence.

If contradictory evidence is irrefutable, a model may need replacement. If evidence is uncertain or compatible with multiple explanations, modification or coexistence of models may be appropriate.
