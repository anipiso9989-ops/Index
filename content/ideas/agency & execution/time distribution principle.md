***
For a fixed amount of total work time, fragmenting that time into more separate sessions can reduce efficiency.

Every session boundary introduces overhead (returning to the task, remembering context, setup/cleanup, etc…). So, if:
$$T_{\text{total}} = T_{\text{work}} + T_{\text{overhead}}$$
then increasing the number of sessions $n$ increases:
$$T_{\text{overhead}} \approx n \cdot C$$
where $C$ is the average transition cost.

There is an optimal degree of temporal distribution for a task. Beyond that point, fragmentation causes diminishing returns and eventually requires additional effort to achieve the same output. So, you can't just do all your work in one big block; some distribution is actually beneficial.

However, this may not be universal. The optimal distribution depends on the task: some tasks benefit from spacing because incubation, consolidation, fatigue reduction, or memory effects outweigh transition costs.

The next steps, then, are to find that optimal degree, and to find how task efficiency varies as a function of temporal distribution, with constant work time.
